Discrete Operational Amplifier Design

LTSpice Execution KiCad PCB Design Analog Circuit Design

Project Overview & Objective

This engineering project was undertaken to translate core semiconductor theory into physical execution. The circuit architecture is based on principles explored within Analysis and Design of Analog Integrated Circuits by Gray, Hurst, Lewis, and Meyer.

It explores the foundational internal topologies of operational amplifiers, specifically analyzing differential input stages, current mirror loads, voltage amplification stages, and class-AB output buffers. To reinforce these fundamentals, the project was designed entirely from discrete components rather than utilizing pre-packaged ICs.

Implementation & Challenges

Initial evaluations on a solderless breadboard exposed expected parasitic capacitance problems and high-frequency noise interference inherent to transient prototyping environments. The long lead lengths created unintended antennas, leading to severe oscillation in the high-gain stages.

To eliminate these layout-induced variables and achieve clean validation results, a custom four-layer integrated PCB was designed in KiCad. This allowed for strict engineering layout constraints, optimizing trace routing to reduce parasitics, and implementing a proper continuous ground-plane to shield against EMI.
